== pattern fast-slow-pointers
complexity: O(n) time · O(1) space
nudge: The answer weaves the front half with the back half reversed. Can you find the middle, reverse the back, and weave, each as its own small step?
-- step Find the middle
1 if the list has fewer than two nodes: return head
  hint: When is there nothing to reorder?
  why: Zero or one node is already in order.
2 slow, fast = head, head.next
  hint: Where do the two pointers start?
  why: fast one ahead, so slow stops at the end of the first half.
3 while fast and fast.next are not None:
  hint: When does fast stop?
  why: When it cannot take two more steps.
4     slow, fast = slow.next, fast.next.next
  hint: How fast does each move?
  why: One and two steps, so slow reaches the middle as fast reaches the end.
-- step Cut and reverse the back half
5 second = slow.next
  hint: Where does the back half start?
  why: Just after the middle.
6 slow.next = None
  hint: What separates the halves?
  why: Ending the first half at the middle.
7 previous = None
  hint: What does the reversed back half end in?
  why: Nothing, as in any reversal.
8 while second is not None:
  hint: How long do you reverse?
  why: Until the back half runs out.
9     following = second.next
  hint: What must you save first?
  why: The next node, before its link is overwritten.
10     second.next = previous
  hint: Which way does this node point now?
  why: Back at the node behind it.
11     previous, second = second, following
  hint: How do you step on?
  why: This node is now behind you, and the saved one is next.
-- step Weave the halves
12 first, second = head, previous
  hint: Which two lists do you weave?
  why: The front half from head, and the reversed back half from previous.
13 while second is not None:
  hint: Which list runs out first?
  why: The back half, which is never longer than the front.
14     first_next, second_next = first.next, second.next
  hint: What do you save before relinking?
  why: Both next nodes, since both links are about to change.
15     first.next = second
  hint: What follows a front node?
  why: The matching back node.
16     second.next = first_next
  hint: What follows that back node?
  why: The next front node.
17     first, second = first_next, second_next
  hint: How do you step on?
  why: To the saved next nodes.
18 return head
  hint: What do you return?
  why: The head, unchanged: the list was reordered in place.
== pattern two-pointers
complexity: O(n) time · O(n) space
nudge: A linked list cannot be read from the back, but an array can. Collect the nodes, then relink from both ends toward the middle.
-- step Collect the nodes
1 nodes = []
  hint: What gives you random access?
  why: An array of the nodes.
2 node = head
  hint: Where does the walk start?
  why: At the head.
3 while node is not None:
  hint: How far do you walk?
  why: To the end of the list.
4     append node to nodes; node = node.next
  hint: What happens at each node?
  why: Store it, then step on.
-- step Relink from both ends
5 low, high = 0, last index
  hint: Where do the pointers start?
  why: At the first and the last node.
6 while low < high:
  hint: When do they stop?
  why: When they meet in the middle.
7     nodes[low].next = nodes[high]
  hint: What follows a front node?
  why: The current back node.
8     low += 1
  hint: Then?
  why: Move the front pointer in.
9     if low == high: stop
  hint: What if the pointers have met?
  why: Every node is linked; stop before linking a node to itself.
10     nodes[high].next = nodes[low]
  hint: What follows a back node?
  why: The next front node.
11     high -= 1
  hint: Then?
  why: Move the back pointer in.
-- step End the list
12 if nodes: nodes[low].next = None
  hint: What must the last node point at?
  why: Nothing. Without this the old link survives and makes a cycle.
13 return head
  hint: What do you return?
  why: The head, reordered in place.
