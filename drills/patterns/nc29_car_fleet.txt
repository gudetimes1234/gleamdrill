== pattern greedy
complexity: O(n log n) time · O(n) space
nudge: A car can only be held up by a car ahead of it. Meet the cars from the one nearest the target backwards: what single fact about the fleet ahead decides whether this car joins it?
-- step Meet the cars front first
1 cars = (position, speed) pairs, sorted by position, nearest the target first
  hint: In what order should you consider the cars?
  why: Nearest the target first, so everything ahead of a car is settled before you reach it.
2 fleets = 0
  hint: What are you counting?
  why: Fleets, none yet.
3 lead_distance, lead_speed = 0, 1
  hint: How do you describe the fleet ahead before there is one?
  why: As arriving at time zero (distance 0 at speed 1), so the first car always starts a fleet.
-- step Does this car catch the fleet ahead?
4 for pos, spd in cars:
  hint: How do you walk the cars?
  why: In the sorted order, once.
5     distance = target - pos
  hint: How far does this car still have to go?
  why: From its position to the target; its arrival time is distance / spd.
6     if distance * lead_speed > lead_distance * spd:
  hint: How do you compare the two arrival times without fractions?
  why: Cross-multiply: distance / spd > lead_distance / lead_speed is the same test in integers. Strictly later means it never catches up.
7         fleets += 1
  hint: What does arriving strictly later mean?
  why: The car heads a fleet of its own.
8         lead_distance, lead_speed = distance, spd
  hint: Which time must the cars behind now beat?
  why: This new fleet's, since it is now the fleet directly ahead of them. A car that catches up changes nothing.
-- step Count
9 return fleets
  hint: What is the answer?
  why: The number of cars that started a new fleet.
