== pattern heap
complexity: O(n log k) time · O(k) space
nudge: You only ever need k candidates. Which heap lets you throw out the farthest of them each time a new point arrives, and do you need the square root to compare distances?
-- step Keep the k nearest so far
1 heap = empty
  hint: What do you keep as you scan the points?
  why: At most k candidates, with the farthest of them on top so it is the first to go.
2 for each point in points:
  hint: How many passes do you need?
  why: One; every point gets a chance to enter the heap.
3     push (-(x² + y²), point)
  hint: What do you push so the farthest point sits on top of a min-heap?
  why: The negated squared distance. The square root cannot change the order, so skip it, and negating turns the min-heap into a max-heap on distance.
4     if the heap holds more than k: pop the top
  hint: How do you keep the heap at size k?
  why: Pop the farthest; it cannot be among the k closest.
-- step Return the points
5 return the point from every entry
  hint: What do you hand back?
  why: The k points left in the heap, without their distances; any order is allowed.
